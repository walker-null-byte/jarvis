# Writing outreach messages that get replies

A first message has one job: start a conversation. It is not a pitch deck.

## Shape (under 80 words)
1. **Hook (1 sentence)**: something specific and true about them. Their game, a recent project, a post, their studio's focus.
   - Good: "Loved the pixel-art lighting in your Monsoon Run devlog."
   - Bad: "I came across your amazing profile!" (could be sent to anyone)
2. **Bridge (1–2 sentences)**: why you're writing to *them* in particular, and the offer in plain words.
3. **Easy question (1 sentence)**: something they can answer in a few words.
   - "Would something like that be useful for your team?"
   - "Are you experimenting with AI tools in your pipeline yet?"
4. **Sign-off** with the sender's first name.

## Rules
- No links in the first message (they look like spam in DMs). Links come in the follow-up.
- No fake familiarity ("as we discussed"), no pressure ("only 2 seats left!") unless it's true and the user asked for it.
- Instagram DMs: casual, short lines, at most one emoji if the tone allows. LinkedIn: a little more formal, full sentences.
- Never invent a detail to sound personal. If nothing specific is known, compliment the kind of work they do instead.

## Follow-up after a reply (under 70 words)
- Thank them and pick up a word or two from the conversation.
- Offer a short call (15–20 min) and give the calendar link.
- Make it easy to say no: "If now's not a good time, no worries at all."
